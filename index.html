How to play

Twenty-nine is a trick-taking game for four players in two partnerships. Partners sit opposite. You score by taking card points in tricks — not by counting tricks.

The cards

A 32-card pack: J 9 A 10 K Q 8 7 in each suit, ranked in that order, highest first. The jack is the top card and the nine is second — it is not ace-high.

  • Card points — jack 3, nine 2, ace 1, ten 1; king, queen, eight and seven nothing. That is 7 a suit and 28 in the pack.

The deal

Everything goes anticlockwise: the next player is the one on your right. The dealer gives four cards to each player, starting on their right; the auction is bid on those four. After trump is chosen, four more each.

The auction

Starting right of the dealer, each player either bids higher than the current bid or passes. Bids run from 16 to 28; a bid is how many card points your side will take. A pass is final for the deal. The bidding ends when a bid stands and the other three have passed, or as soon as someone bids 28. If all four pass, the deal is thrown in and the next dealer deals.

The hidden trump

The highest bidder places one of their four cards face down as the trump indicator. Its suit is trump, and nobody else knows it — not even their partner. The indicator is out of the bidder's hand until trump is revealed, then goes back into it.

Playing

The player right of the dealer leads. You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot and trump is still hidden, you choose:

  • Play without calling — any card. It is a plain discard, whatever its suit.
  • Call for trump — the indicator is turned up for everyone. You must then play a trump if you hold one; otherwise anything.

Once trump is face up, a player who cannot follow may play any card. A card's role is fixed when it is played: a trump-suit card played before the reveal stays a plain card. The highest trump played at or after the reveal wins the trick; if there is none, the highest card of the suit led. If nobody ever calls, the indicator is the bidder's last card, and playing it reveals the trump.

Scoring

The bidding side counts the card points in every trick either partner won. With at least their bid they score +1 game point; otherwise −1. Only the bidding side's game points move, so scores go negative. The first side to +6 wins; a side that falls to −6 loses.

Options the host can switch on

  • Pair — Once trump has been revealed, a player holding the King and Queen of trump can declare the pair when their side wins a trick. If the bidding side holds it, the bid goes down 4 (never below 16); if the opponents hold it, the bid goes up 4 (never above 28). Once per deal.
  • Double — Before the first lead, each opponent of the bidder may double, and if they do, the bidding side may redouble. Doubled, the deal is worth 2 game points; redoubled, 4.
  • Single hand — After the last four cards, any player may declare single hand: a promise to win all eight tricks alone. The bid is void and there is no trump; the declarer leads and their partner sits out. Winning every trick scores 3 game points; losing any trick costs 3 and ends the deal at once.
  • Seventh card — Instead of choosing, the bidder may announce seventh card: the seventh card dealt to them becomes the face-down indicator, unseen by everyone — the bidder included.

Playing together

One person hosts and shares the four-character room code; everyone else joins with it. Empty seats are filled by bots. The connection is directly between your devices — there is no server holding the game.

You can leave whenever you like — the ✕ in the strip, or LEAVE under the scores. Your seat is kept, and a bot plays your cards until you are back. Rejoin with the same code on the same device and you get your seat and your full scoreboard back. The same goes if your phone dies or your browser closes.

The host's device is the one the game runs on. If the host leaves, the match stops for everyone until they press RESUME on their home screen; then everybody rejoins with the same code and it carries on from the same card.
